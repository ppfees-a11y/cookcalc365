Calculation Methodology & Reference Standards

How CookCalc365 calculates unit conversions and handles ingredient uncertainty.

Volume conversion

All supported volume units convert first to milliliters. One US customary cup equals 236.5882365 mL, a US tablespoon equals one sixteenth of a cup, and a US teaspoon equals one forty-eighth of a cup. Results are calculated at full internal precision and rounded only for display.

Mass conversion

One international avoirdupois pound is exactly 453.59237 grams. One ounce is one sixteenth of a pound, or 28.349523125 grams. Kilograms contain exactly 1,000 grams. Weight conversion does not use ingredient density because it is a mass-to-mass operation.

Ingredient volume to mass

For a selected ingredient, the calculator stores a reference grams-per-US-cup value. Cups multiplied by grams per cup produce grams. To convert back, grams are divided by the same reference. This approach is useful for planning, but its output remains an estimate due to packing, brand and moisture differences.

Ingredient sources and limitations

Reference values are informed by the published King Arthur Baking ingredient weight chart for flour, sugar, butter, honey, oats, cocoa and other common foods. Certain everyday values such as milk, rice and oil are labeled approximate. Published charts can differ, so recipe-specific measurements and a kitchen scale take priority. See the source link below for independent verification.

Temperature conversion

Celsius equals (Fahrenheit minus 32) multiplied by five ninths. Fahrenheit equals Celsius multiplied by nine fifths plus 32. This converts temperature scales only; it does not predict heating time, convection performance or the actual temperature inside an oven.

Recipe scaling and rounding

The scaling factor is desired servings divided by original servings. Each original ingredient amount is multiplied by this factor. Invalid zero or negative serving counts are rejected. Decimal results are displayed for clarity, while the user remains responsible for translating them into practical measuring spoon fractions.

Further reading and references

For independently published ingredient weights and measurement technique, see King Arthur Baking’s ingredient weight chart and its recipe success guide. Use our calculator tools to explore the relationships described above.

How the new tools calculate results

Whole-recipe scaling reads a leading quantity on each line and multiplies it by target servings divided by original servings. The parser supports whole numbers, decimals, common fraction forms and mixed numbers. It preserves the remainder of the line without inferring ingredient density. Lines without a recognizable leading number are left unchanged. Fractions are displayed when the result closely matches a sixteenth; otherwise results are rounded to three decimal places.

Pan area uses π × diameter² ÷ 4 for circles and width × length for rectangles. Cost per serving equals (ingredient costs + additional costs) ÷ serving count. All computations happen in your browser; no recipe is uploaded to a server.
