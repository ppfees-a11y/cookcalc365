Recipe Conversion Checklist

A practical checklist for adapting recipes before cooking.

Before you change the yield

Record the original serving count and the desired count. For each ingredient, identify whether the quantity is a weight, a volume, or a count. A recipe calling for 200 g of flour should be scaled as mass, not converted through a generic cup weight. Keep the original instructions available for comparison.

Scale the ingredient list

Calculate new amount = original amount × desired servings ÷ original servings. For example, increasing four servings to six multiplies each scalable ingredient by 1.5. A 2-cup broth quantity becomes 3 cups; 300 g of beans becomes 450 g. Eggs and packaged ingredients may require practical rounding.

Check what should not scale automatically

Cooking time, pan dimensions, oven temperature, salt, hot spices, leavening, and thickening may need adjustment based on the recipe and method. Doubling the batter in the same pan makes a deeper layer; it does not imply twice the baking time. For baked goods, prefer a tested recipe at the target yield.

Confirm measuring conventions

A US customary cup is approximately 236.6 mL; a metric cup in some recipes is 250 mL. An ingredient cup-to-gram estimate depends on the ingredient and measuring method. Check whether a recipe uses packed brown sugar, sifted flour, or spooned-and-leveled flour.

Run the final review

Verify the total amount fits your equipment. Check the ingredient list against the scaled amounts and review the budget per serving. When adjusting an unfamiliar recipe, write down the actual results so future batches can use a tested conversion.

These examples are general educational estimates. Follow the original recipe and food-safety guidance for specific ingredients and cooking methods.
